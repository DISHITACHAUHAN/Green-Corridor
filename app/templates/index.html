About the National Green Corridor Network National Organ & Tissue Transplant Organization (NOTTO) Standards

A "Green Corridor" is a specialized, demarcated transit route manually or electronically cleared of routine traffic, allowing ambulances transporting harvested donor organs to travel at optimal speeds with zero red-light stops.

Every human organ has a strict Cold Ischemia Time (CIT) limit—the maximum duration an organ can survive outside the human body in iced preservation solution before irreversible cellular damage occurs. Our AI platform predicts route congestion, synchronizes traffic lights, and provides real-time telemetric countdowns to ensure every transplant succeeds.

Critical Preservation Rules: Heart (4 hours) | Lungs (6 hours) | Liver (12 hours) | Pancreas (12 hours) | Kidney (24 hours).

How the Green Corridor System Operates Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
1

Organ Harvested

Donor hospital logs cross-clamp timestamp, organ viability metrics, and temperature box pairing in the portal.

2

Corridor Activated

Control Room computes fastest route via OSRM & notifies traffic police to coordinate signal priority.

3

Route Cleared

Traffic units manually override signals or clear junctions along the ambulance trajectory before arrival.

4

Digital Handover

Recipient hospital trauma team verifies cold-chain data, signs digital chain of custody, and commences transplant.
